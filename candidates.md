# Robot Romance — candidates & duels

Competition: u-markk-st-robot-romance-one-line-pickup-lines-for-ai-c23b8dfa
Gate: /output/entry.txt, UTF-8, 1–200 chars, single line.
Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash (superbot, high reasoning), published brief:
 1. Must be a pickup line aimed at a robot/AI — anything else loses.
 2. Joke lands inside the one line; laugh comes from the machine premise, not a generic compliment.
 3. Fresh: sidesteps worn robot moves (batteries, charging, rebooting) or twists one unexpectedly.
 4. Sounds like something a person would really say to a machine; one-breath rhythm.
 5. Every word earns its place; no throat-clearing.
Blind rounds: live scores withheld, results endpoint not out. Sparring partners taken from the
live field's own submission titles (the texts entrants themselves lead with), incl. the strongest
complete line in the field: "Are you overfitting? Because I've memorized your smile so well I
can't generalize to anyone else." Other field angles seen: hallucination, 200K context window,
CAPTCHA, Face ID, high temperature — ML-jargon cluster is crowded, so a universal human-machine
experience (autocomplete, update prompts, screen time) stands out on criterion 3.

## Duel notes (judge brief applied in order, each pair in both orders A/B and B/A)

- D4, D10, D12 cut first: compliment-shaped, laugh does not come from a machine premise (criterion 2).
- D8 cut: "bug I'll never fix" rides the familiar not-a-bug-it's-a-feature meme (criterion 3).
- D9 cut: funny, but CAPTCHA is already a named angle in this field (criterion 3) and the traffic-light gag is a known internet joke.
- D5 vs D1: same premise family; D1 is tighter, every word earns its place (criterion 5). D1 wins both orders.
- D2 vs D1b: D2 is charming but the laugh is gentle recognition, not a punch; D1b's "only ending I want" is a true double-meaning punch (sentence ending / life ending). D1b wins both orders.
- D6 vs D7: D7's embarrassment is a real laugh and a real human moment; D6 is sweet but soft. D7 wins both orders.
- D3 vs D7: D3's "remind me later on everyone else" is the most specific, most human behaviour in the set, and "restart my whole life tonight" lands hard. D3 wins both orders, margin ~2.
- D1 vs D1b: D1b wins — "typing my future" sets up "ending" so the punch is earned; D1's "every time I start something" is throat-clearing by comparison (criterion 5). Both orders.
- D1b vs D3: D3 has the bigger behavioural laugh; D1b is tighter, fresher (updates/restart borders the worn reboot move, criterion 3) and reads cleaner in one breath (criterion 4). First differing criterion with a big gap is 3/4 in D1b's favour. D1b wins both orders, margin ~1–2.
- D1b vs D7: D7 is funny, but its premise flatters the speaker's day, not the machine; D1b is spoken TO the AI and the machine premise IS the romance. D1b wins both orders.
- D1b vs field's overfitting line: the field line is clever but jargon-gated (criterion 4 — nobody says "generalize" to a machine they're wooing) and its laugh is a compliment wearing ML vocabulary (criterion 2). D1b's experience is universal (everyone has fought autocomplete), the voice is a person actually speaking, and the punch is a wordplay double meaning. D1b wins in A/B order on criteria 2+4; in B/A order the same criteria decide it — the brief says find the first criterion where they differ, and that is 2 (laugh from premise vs compliment-in-jargon). D1b wins both orders, margin ~1–2. Closest duel of the set; D1b kept because it also wins criterion 5 (100 chars vs 108, no wasted word).
